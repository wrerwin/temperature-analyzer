Methods & data

Baseline. Every comparison is against the baseline years (default: earliest record to 2000). For each calendar day the normal and its σ come from the baseline years' values for that day, pooled over ±7 days (±3 when smoothing). When a baseline year is itself being scored it is left out of its own normal.

Day. Where the selected day's value sits in the baseline distribution for that calendar day: z-score, percentile, and every year's value for the same day.

Year. Each day's departure from normal for one year, with the ±1σ / ±2σ bands; smoothing averages the actual and the baseline years alike so the bands stay honest.

Decades. For every year, the number of days beyond +1σ / +2σ / +3σ (hot) and −1σ / −2σ / −3σ (cold), counted separately so they can't cancel; years with fewer than 300 valid days are faded.

Volatility. Spread: the standard deviation of the year's daily z-scores around that year's own mean, so a uniformly warm year is not "volatile" — only swings are. Day-to-day swings: the root-mean-square change in z between consecutive days. Both are divided by their average over the baseline years, so 1.0 means as volatile as the baseline.

Map. Precomputed for 1 in 100 contiguous-US zip codes, each mapped to the nearest NOAA station with a long, well-covered record (see analysis/ in the repository); same definitions as above with a fixed 1940–2000 baseline. Dot colors use one scale across all years so the animation is comparable.

Data. Station history: NOAA GHCN-Daily via NCEI (nearest station within ~35 mi with the longest well-covered record; daily mean = (high + low) / 2; lags ~3 days). Reanalysis: ERA5 via the Open-Meteo archive (1940–present, ~5 day lag), typically a degree or two cooler on daily highs than a station. Recent days and today: Open-Meteo forecast. Geocoding: zippopotam.us. Today's high may still rise until evening.
